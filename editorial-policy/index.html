Editorial policy

The Gutcast Blog publishes practical guides for people who want to understand the connection between what they eat and how their gut feels. It is written by the Gutcast Editorial Team at Rewire & Rise Education Pty Ltd, the company that makes the Gutcast app for iPhone.

How we write articles

  • We start from real questions. Topics come from the questions people actually type into search engines and app stores about bloating, trigger foods, food diaries and digestive symptoms.
  • We check facts against primary sources. Medical and nutrition claims are checked against government health agencies (such as the U.S. National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases), academic medical centers, university research groups (such as Monash University's FODMAP team), clinical guidelines and peer-reviewed research. Every article lists its sources at the end.
  • We use AI tools in our process. Articles are researched and drafted with the help of AI writing tools, then checked against the sources they cite before they are published. We never invent studies, statistics, quotes or expert credentials.
  • We keep the language careful. We write about patterns and possibilities, not diagnoses. Food reactions are personal, and the same food can affect two people very differently.
  • We update articles. When guidance changes or we find an error, we correct the article and change its "Updated" date.

What our articles are not

Gutcast articles are general wellness information. They are not medical advice and do not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition. Gutcast is a wellness pattern-tracking app, not a medical device.

Talk with a doctor or a registered dietitian about ongoing digestive symptoms, before starting a restrictive diet, and before removing gluten if celiac disease has not been ruled out. Seek care promptly for red-flag symptoms such as blood in your stool, unexplained weight loss, fever, persistent vomiting, difficulty swallowing, anemia, or severe, worsening or persistent pain or bloating.

Our bias

We make the Gutcast app, and many articles explain how it can help you track meals and symptoms. We say so plainly, and we only recommend the app where food-and-symptom tracking is genuinely useful.

Corrections and feedback

Spotted an error or have a question about an article? Contact us through the Gutcast support page.
